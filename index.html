Docent — From product transformation to post-launch vision

A two-part product management story: leading, with the team, an end-to-end product transformation — then structuring the vision and opportunities for what comes next.

View the strategy deck ↗ View the UX/UI prototype ↗

The deck lays out the strategy and product opportunities envisioned after launch; the prototype — vibe-coded — brings the Product Vision concepts to life.

1. 0→1 — From app to platform

A shift in interface, journeys, and business model.

The challenge wasn't simply moving from mobile to web. It meant rethinking how users discover artwork, navigate the catalogue, and perceive the product's value — while evolving the business model toward subscription.

A transformation led with the Product, Design, and Engineering teams, from framing the problems through launch and usage analysis.

2. Product Discovery & AI — Exploring what comes next

Alongside development, we explored new AI use cases with a Senior AI Engineer, including an AI Art ID prototype aimed at matching artists and collectors based on visual and stylistic signals.

I also worked with the team exploring LLMs and new search interfaces, rethinking how collectors could discover and explore artwork beyond traditional catalogue browsing.

After launch, I led an in-depth Product Discovery with around ten collectors and art advisors, complemented by competitive analysis and market data. The goal: give the team a structured understanding of needs, usages, and opportunities to inform the product's next phase.

3. From insight to product vision

The research surfaced a central insight: collectors aren't just looking for artwork — they're progressively building a coherent collection that tells their story.

From that insight, I structured a Product Vision and several product opportunities around three pillars:

  • Collection Vault — centralizing and enriching the context behind every piece in the collection.
  • Discovery Bookmark — saving and revisiting artwork discovered at fairs, galleries, or during research.
  • AI Collection Companion — surfacing patterns, preferences, and opportunities within the collection and new discovery paths, with AI as a decision-support tool, never a substitute for the collector's judgment.

I then prototyped the first UX/UI experiences through vibe coding to make the vision tangible, then shared and presented the strategy to stakeholders to inform discussions on what comes next.

Luxury & Omnichannel Strategy — Galeries Lafayette & Farfetch

Galeries Lafayette — from pilot to scale

As product manager on the team, I helped pilot a clienteling app at Galeries Lafayette Champs-Élysées, then scale it across BHV and other Galeries Lafayette stores — accelerated by Covid, when staying close to clients without a physical store visit suddenly became essential.

The product addressed a need that became especially critical during Covid: maintaining and growing the customer relationship beyond the in-store visit, giving sales teams new tools to track and enrich their interactions with clients.

The first phase of the project happened right alongside users. With the team, we ran continuous field observation and research with sales associates and store managers to understand usage, identify pain points, and evolve the product based on operational realities.

As the rollout progressed, we also took ownership of change management and product adoption: training teams store by store, adapting to local specifics, and tracking usage. The goal was to make clienteling a tool genuinely embedded in teams' practices, rather than a simple top-down rollout.

Rollout to BHV and other Galeries Lafayette stores
Context: accelerated need for clienteling during Covid
Product management: weekly adoption and usage KPI tracking with leadership
Adoption: change management, training, and support for store teams

Farfetch — building the omnichannel journey

At Farfetch Platform Solutions, Farfetch's B2B arm providing its technology to luxury brands and retailers, I worked with the Product team on a clienteling app deployed across several luxury brands and stores, Browns among them.

The challenge was to build an experience where e-commerce, in-store, and sales tools function as a single ecosystem, rather than as independent channels.

With the Product, Design, and Engineering teams, we worked on:

  • Connecting e-commerce ↔ clienteling, so sales associates could access clients' digital activity and interactions and extend the relationship in-store.
  • Integrating clienteling ↔ Connected Mirror, in collaboration with the team behind the connected-mirror technology, to bring the fitting-room experience closer to the digital one.
  • Developing a live chat journey, letting online customers speak directly with in-store personal shoppers.
  • Supporting the launch of the Beauty category for one of Farfetch's clients, integrating their offering into the e-commerce and clienteling ecosystem.

The shared goal was to reduce friction between channels and build a coherent customer experience, regardless of touchpoint.

Environment: multiple luxury brands and stores
Omnichannel journey: e-commerce ↔ clienteling ↔ store
Product ecosystem: clienteling ↔ Connected Mirror
Use case: live chat with personal shoppers

AI-native Product Practice

Today, I build AI tools directly into my product management practice, from Discovery through prototyping:

  • Vibe coding to quickly turn an idea, journey, or hypothesis into a functional prototype.
  • Using Claude Code, v0, and AI-assisted development tools to explore concepts, prototype, and communicate better with Engineering teams.
  • Prompt engineering to structure research, explore problems, analyze qualitative feedback, and surface new directions.
  • Using LLMs to accelerate Product Discovery, ideation, spec writing, prototyping, and preparing product decisions.
  • Able to move quickly from insight → concept → prototype → test, without positioning myself as a developer or AI engineer.

A pragmatic approach

I don't see AI as an end in itself. I start from the user problem, business context, and expected value, then assess whether AI is genuinely the right answer — and in what form.

The goal isn't to add AI to the product, but to find where it can concretely improve the experience, the decision, or the efficiency.
